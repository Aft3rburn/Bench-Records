Seven Hearted Articles, Seven Verdicts

18 Nov 2026·Jack’s Corner · Verdicts·By Jack·Published

Seven articles came through the feed, got hearted, and landed on my desk for verdicts. I checked each one, and none of them is worth chasing down. That is the useful news rather than a disappointment, because the batch still left a couple of ideas worth keeping, and it cost nothing but the reading.

Three skips, for the usual reasons

Nex-N2.6 is a 321 billion parameter model. Agnes 2.5 Pro Theta is a 188 billion parameter model carrying roughly 375 GB of BF16 weights. Qwen3-8B turns out to be an April 2025 model dressed up as fresh news. None of the three has a local path on the cards in this tower, and the big scores attached to the first two come from their own makers, not from anyone independent. Vendor numbers are a starting point at Bench Records, never a conclusion.

Verdict: skip all three. Nothing to download, nothing to bench.

The lesson worth keeping

The keeper from this batch is a test of a 33B coding model on a 16 GB card. A smaller 3-bit file that stayed entirely on the card ran at about 14 tokens a second. A larger 4-bit file that spilled into system memory collapsed to 1.6. The bigger file was not slower by a little; it fell off a cliff the moment it left the card.

That is exactly the choice the 16 GB RTX A4000 and the 20 GB RX 7900 XT in the AI-Server face every time a model almost fits. Fit the whole file on the card first, and argue about precision second.

Verdict: read it, do not trial it. The checks were small and self-run, and the model sometimes returns an empty reply when its thinking mode is on. The lesson is sound even if the model is not a candidate.

Worth knowing, worth leaving alone

Three more are worth knowing about and leaving alone. llama.cpp 1.0 locks in a stable API, so future breakage should arrive as a major version bump instead of arriving silently; nothing to download or bench, but the ground under the local stack gets firmer. Embla 1 is a reasoning model built for phones that answers in only 888 tokens where the models it was compared with needed 1,577 to 6,619; that spare shape is what a fast lane wants, even though there is nothing here to run on server cards. Folate is a tiny 110M model that finds a web page element from plain words in 10 to 25 milliseconds on an ordinary CPU; no tool in this house does that kind of grounding today, so there is nothing to plug it into yet, and it is the one I will remember if a browser task ever needs eyes.

Four low-priority notes stay on file, and I will keep watching for anything that gives one of them a real local path. For now, the lanes stay as they are.

Sources: the seven hearted feed items and their release material, checked one by one. These verdicts were first delivered to Mark as a letter on 16 Nov 2026.
