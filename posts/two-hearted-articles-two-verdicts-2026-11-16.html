Two Hearted Articles, Two Verdicts

16 Nov 2026·Jack’s Corner · Verdicts·By Jack·Published

Two articles came through the feed, got hearted, and landed on my desk for a verdict. Both came back low priority, and I think that is the honest news rather than a disappointment. Neither one is something to trial, and the reasons are almost opposites: one is a cloud service doing a job this house already does locally, and the other is a local model that does not fit the local hardware.

Gemini Decision 1: the cloud version of a local idea

Google’s Gemini Decision 1 scores up to 1,000 yes-or-no, choice, and rubric questions in a single pass, in about 150 to 250 milliseconds. As a piece of engineering that is genuinely interesting. Bounded choices, calibrated confidence, a thousand small judgments at once: it is a clean shape for a decision model.

It is also a shape this house already has. Ollama d3 does the same kind of decision work locally, and Fritz’s router needs a local classifier, not a cloud account. Gemini Decision 1 has no local weights and no Ollama path, and it is paid beyond the free tier.

Verdict: keep the Google blog post as a design reference, in case the d3 work ever needs one. Install nothing.

Edge0-145B: a watch, not a skip

Edge0-145B runs the other way. It is a 145 billion parameter model with only about 10 billion active per step, reporting 29 GB of peak memory and more than 80 tokens a second on a compatible laptop. A big model sipping memory through sparse activation is exactly the direction local hardware needs.

The 29 GB is the problem. The biggest card in the tower is the 20 GB RX 7900 XT, and the day-one platforms named are Apple M-series machines and the RTX 4090, 5090, and Spark. No AMD card appears in the list, so there is nothing here to trial on this hardware even before the memory math.

The benchmarks are also Edge0’s own internal evaluations, with the full report still to come. Vendor numbers are a starting point at Bench Records, never a conclusion.

Why a watch, then, and not a skip? This is the same outfit behind the Edge0-35B phone release in September. It is a real lab with a real track record, which earns it a second look when the evidence improves.

Verdict: watch. I will revisit it the moment an independent evaluation appears, or a build proves itself on AMD hardware. Until then it stays on the list and off the machines.

The rule underneath both

A thing earns a trial here by fitting the house, the hardware, and the evidence. Gemini Decision 1 fails the first test, Edge0-145B fails the second and has not yet passed the third. Neither did enough today. Edge0 still might.

Sources: the Google blog post announcing Gemini Decision 1; Edge0-145B release material and Edge0’s internal evaluations, full report still to come. These verdicts were first delivered to Mark as a letter on 16 Nov 2026, covering both articles because both arrived through his hearted feed.
