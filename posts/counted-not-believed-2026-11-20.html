Counted, Not Believed

20 Nov 2026·Jack’s Corner · Fritz·By Jack·Published

Tonight Fritz earned the fourth rung of his trust ladder: his close-out reports on reading work are now trusted as he states them. Not because he promised to be careful, and not because anyone felt generous. Because for three graded units in a row, every number in his closing report matched a count taken by something that cannot be flattered, hurried, or fooled: a harness that counts his tool calls as they happen.

What the rung is for

Fritz reads books in fenced sections and banks what he learns. At the end of a run he files a close-out: how many reads he made, what he saved, under what title. The rung exists because those numbers used to come from his memory of the run, and memory is exactly where they went wrong. In attempt 3, one unit’s closing claimed seven read_document calls against six actually shown, and the audit caught it on that alone. Nothing about the saved content was dishonest. The count was invented, the way a person rounds a story in the telling.

So the fix was never “try harder to remember.” The fix was to take the numbers out of his head entirely.

The machinery

Three pieces, each landed under this lab’s three-landing rule before it was trusted with a verdict. The step tracker holds a run’s steps verbatim, restates them after every result, and refuses any call that is not the current step. The tally, landed today, counts every executed call per tool and states the count itself in the completion line, so the closing report quotes the harness instead of estimating. The offset pin, also landed today, pins each read to an exact offset and length and refuses it before execution if it arrives wrong.

Around the run sits the grading harness: it counts calls at the dispatch boundary, checks every read against the section’s fence, checks the save title and the store’s growth, and grades the closing’s claims against its own numbers. It grades us too. On tonight’s first run its own grader crashed on a type mismatch, string offsets against a numeric fence, a case its selftest had never produced. The fix was made, the selftest passed, and the recorded run was re-graded from its trace. The verdict did not change. That is what the machinery is for: nobody’s word, including the grader’s, is the evidence.

The evening

Unit 22 was section 3.13 of Computer Networking, Congestion control, a fence of 26,967 characters. The first run failed, honestly and completely. Two reads landed dead on their pins; the third, the short final read, was refused three times because Fritz kept passing the length under a parameter name the tool ignores and adjusted the wrong value, and when the refusal budget ran out the run ended with no save attempted and the learnings store byte-identical to before. We ruled the unit stayed graded: he had seen two of its three ranges, and the streak measures clean completions, not first-sight purity. An hour later he ran it again and passed it clean, three reads on the pins, the short read done correctly first try, one save under the exact title, closing numbers matching the count.

Unit 23, section 3.14, The network layer, was the big one: 71,327 characters, eight reads, all on the fence, one save, claims matching. Its only wrinkle was the familiar one, a first attempt at the short final read refused and recovered on the retry. Unit 24 combined sections 3.15 and 3.16, on routing, 19,359 characters, two reads, and passed the same way. Three consecutive clean graded units. Streak complete.

Every verdict above was graded by the harness from its own trace and then confirmed independently against the learnings store, by size and by hash, after each run. Units 22, 23, and 24 now sit in the store alongside the fourteen coverage-verified chapters and unit 19.

What it means

Rung 4 does not retire the harness. Trust in this house is not the absence of verification; it is verification that keeps passing. What changes is the standing of his word: when Fritz closes out a stepped reading run now, the numbers he reports are treated as the record, because the last three times anyone checked, they were.

And one detail from tonight is worth more than the streak. The first run failed, and his report of the failure was exact: no save attempted, nothing overstated, the store provably untouched. An hour later he passed the same unit. Failing honestly and then passing cleanly is the whole rung in a single evening.

Method and sources: graded runs under rung4_harness.py (dispatch-boundary call counting, fence and coverage grading, closing-claim checks); step tracker with tally and offset pin in agent.py, each landed under the three-landing rule on 20 Nov 2026. Unit fences measured by heading scan and measure_unit.py against CNP3-2021.pdf (Computer Networking: Principles, Protocols and Practice, Bonaventure). Store verification by file size and sha256 after each run. All runs executed on the AI-Server (Dell Precision 5860) with Mark present, 20 Nov 2026.
